Upgrades

The note under each car is a reading of that published tune. It is not a claim that every catalog part is fitted. The sections after that are general Horizon advice.

1988 Chevrolet Monte Carlo Super Sport

What this tune is asking for

The tune makes 1,004 hp and 2.950 s to 60 mph in a 2,781 lb body, far beyond a stock late-1980s Super Sport. That points to engine power, from displacement or boost, and a drivetrain that can take it. Braking from 60 mph takes 85.10 ft, only mid-pack of these three, so brakes are the weak side. The tires still have to put 1,004 hp down; wider driven rubber matters more than another camshaft.

2017 Acura NSX

What this tune is asking for

Stock, the 2017 NSX is a hybrid of about 600 hp that reaches 60 mph in a little over three seconds. This tune is 853 hp and 2.450 s, so the straight-line step is real. It is also the heaviest of the three at 3,668 lb, and the least grippy: 1.21 g at 60 mph and 1.32 g at 120 mph. Power is ahead of the tires and suspension. The next useful upgrades are compound, width, and springs, not more boost.

2011 BMW X5 M Forza Edition

What this tune is asking for

The stock X5 M is about 547 hp and takes roughly 4.7 s to reach 62 mph. The Forza Edition tune is 1,124 hp, 3.430 s to 60 mph, and 223.60 mph, with the shortest stops here (71.70 ft from 60 mph) and the highest lateral grip (2.29 g at 120 mph). Weight is 2,822 lb, which a road X5 is not, so this build is power, brakes, and weight reduction together.

General Horizon advice

Tires and rims

The car's power only counts if the tires can put it on the road. A wider contact patch and a softer compound add grip and wear faster. Larger, lighter rims cut unsprung weight. Low-profile sidewalls keep the tread planted when the load shifts.

Drivetrain

Clutch, gearbox, driveshaft, and differential decide how that power arrives. A race transmission holds the extra torque. A limited-slip differential keeps both driven wheels working in a corner. Upgrade the path to the wheels before chasing another hundred horsepower the stock clutch cannot hold.

Brakes

More speed needs a way to rein it in. Shorter stops come from better pads, larger rotors, and tires that can use them. Of these three tunes, the X5 M Forza Edition already stops shortest. The Monte Carlo Super Sport is the one that still gives up distance.

Suspension

Springs, dampers, and anti-roll bars trade comfort for a tire that stays flat on the road. Stiffer is not always faster. The NSX tune's low lateral g is the clue to start here, after the tires.
